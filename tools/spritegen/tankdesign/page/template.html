03 · The four lines

One language per line

Each line redraws the whole roster in one language, so one can carry the game on its own, or you can mix them per chassis. Every silhouette should read on its own inside a line.

04 · Damage and wrecks

Four live tiers, four ways to die

Damage is planned once per tank and grows tier by tier, so a scratch taken at a quarter health is still there at the end. Burning swaps a plate's paint for char but keeps its shading, so a wreck still reads as the tank it was. Shown for the tank in the inspector.

New
Today
  • Pristine (0–24 % damage): as built.
  • Scuffed (25–49 %): scratches to bare metal, a scorch, a dent, one lamp cracked, loose stowage gone.
  • Damaged (50–74 %): an armour plate blown off to show the frame and a cable, a penetration hole, antennas snapped, half the lamps out, sparks at the wound.
  • Critical (75–99 %): more plates gone and fire (or plasma) inside, a broken track run, every light dead but a blinking red warning lamp, the silhouette chipped.
  • Blown: the turret ring torn out and the turret thrown clear, embers in the hole.
  • Gutted: armour stripped, still burning inside.
  • Husk: cold and burnt out, the end state of every wreck.
  • Cook-off: the deck torn open by the ammunition, a whole track run gone.
  • Today the game has two live tiers (30 and 75 damage) and four wrecks; the new ladder adds a tier and keeps the tracks rolling at every live tier.
05 · Weapon systems

Five modules, each on its own hardpoint

The game's five weapon pickups become hardware bolted to the turret. Each module has its own mount point, so a tank carrying two of them shows both rather than stacking them, and each one animates through the states the game already tracks. Shown on the tank in the inspector, firing on a loop.

06 · Lights

Every tank has a light signature

At night the game multiplies the field down to moonlight and adds every light back. The new sprites carry an emissive layer on top: lamps, marker strips, sensor eyes, grav pads, reactors. Enemies glow warm with a red eye; a player's markers and sensor take the team colour, so you can find yourself in the dark.

09 · Shipping it

What the engine needs for these sprites

None of this changes how a tank plays: hit boxes, guns and speeds stay as they are. It is what implementing the picks involves, so you can weigh a line's cost as well as its look.

  • Sheets. 40-pixel cells instead of 32, generated by tools/spritegen/tankdesign: per team, 16 hull cells (4 tiers × 4 track frames), 4 wrecks, 12 turret cells (4 tiers × rest and two recoil poses) and the broken turret, each with a light cell, plus 19 module cells. The extra 8 pixels are room for barrels, antennas and modules; the hull boxes stay as they are.
  • Damage. Three thresholds (25, 50, 75 %) instead of two, and the tracks keep rolling at every live tier; the hull column is tier × 4 + frame.
  • Recoil. The turret shows its recoil cell for a tenth of a second after each barrel fires.
  • Light layer. Drawn additively in the glowing half of the field, after the night multiply, the way shots already are.
  • Lights. Headlight cones start at each design's own lamps; a turret searchlight follows the aim; marker and sensor lights take the team colour.
  • Modules. One layer each at its own hardpoint; bullets, beams and flame leave from the module's muzzle rather than the pivot line.
  • Hover and pods. Skimmers get a longer shadow and a faint wash for a ground mark; Prototype pods leave four thin strips. Tracked lines keep today's marks.
  • Palette. Two interpolated darks join the tank sheets for the blue and teal hulls, and the two team ramps gain one step at each end; the sheet checker learns both.
