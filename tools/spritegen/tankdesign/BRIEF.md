# Tank redesign study: the brief

The owner of bongbong (a fast, fun, pixelated top-down tank shooter with
semi-realistic physics) asked for every tank to be rebuilt: **more detailed,
more visually interesting, more sci-fi, very polished**. Each of the 12
chassis gets **4 upgraded variations**, one per *design line* below, so the
owner can pick a line per tank or one line for the whole roster. The owner
decided:

- **Same size, same 2-px grid.** Hulls keep today's footprints (hit boxes,
  corridor fit); detail comes from design, not resolution. A design pixel is
  drawn 2x2 in the game.
- **Keep each chassis's colour identity** (body + accent families below, on
  the Puny palette). Variations differ in form, detail and livery marks, not
  hue.
- Tanks now have **lights** (the night/weather pass draws headlight cones
  from the nose, a glow in the team colour, and multiplies the field dark),
  can carry **weapon modules** (minigun, seeker-missile pod, plasma, laser,
  flamethrower - the game's pickups) and take **damage in degrees** (four
  live tiers and four wreck variants, generated by the kit from your tags).

The page these designs go into will be looked at closely and designs will be
*chosen*, then implemented. Treat each tank as a hero asset.

## The toolkit (tools/spritegen/tankdesign)

- `kit.py` - palette, materials (`Mat`), chassis table (`CHASSIS`), shape
  helpers, `Part` and shading modes, `Builder`, `Design`, `Line`.
- `damage.py` - the damage/wreck engine (owned by the lead, do not edit).
- `render.py` - previews and the build (do not edit).
- `zoom.py` - one design at a pixel-grid zoom.
- `lines/<line>.py` - **your file**. The assault in each line is a reference
  drawn by the lead: keep its language, improve it if you can.

Run everything under nix's Pillow from this directory:

    nix-shell -p "python3.withPackages (ps: [ps.pillow])" --run "python3 render.py hero <line> <chassis> [...] --zoom 6 --states today,new,p1,p2,turn,night,damaged,critical,blown,gutted,husk,cookoff,mods"
    nix-shell -p "python3.withPackages (ps: [ps.pillow])" --run "python3 zoom.py <line> <chassis> 14"
    nix-shell -p "python3.withPackages (ps: [ps.pillow])" --run "python3 render.py preview <line>"            # whole line, game scale
    nix-shell -p "python3.withPackages (ps: [ps.pillow])" --run "python3 render.py cells <line> <chassis>"   # every cell raw

Outputs land in `target/tankdesign/` (or `TANKDESIGN_OUT`): `hero_<line>.png`,
`zoom_<line>_<chassis>.png`, `preview_<line>.png`, `cells_<line>_<chassis>.png`;
look at them with the Read tool. `today` in the hero view is the current sprite - beat it.

### Coordinates

Design pixels relative to the pivot: x, y in [-20, 19], the pivot between
pixels -1 and 0, y down, **the gun points to -y**. A symmetric part is
`sym(px)` (mirror maps x to -1 - x). `circle(cx, cy, r)`/`ellipse` take
continuous centres (pixel x spans [x, x+1), so the pivot is (0, 0)).

### Shapes

`rect(x0,y0,x1,y1)` (inclusive), `chamfer(..., tl, tr, br, bl)`, `circle`,
`ellipse`, `ring(cx,cy,r_out,r_in)`, `poly([(x,y)...])` (continuous
vertices), `line(x0,y0,x1,y1)`, `mask(rows, x0, y0)`, `mirror`, `sym`,
`shift`, `grow`, `shrink`, `edge`, `bbox`, `rotate_px`. Sets compose with
`|`, `-`, `&`.

### Parts and shading

`b.part(px, mat, mode, z, step=0, bevel=1, tags=(), name=None, normal=None,
gain=1.0, center=None, pattern={}, sep=None, contact=True, cast=True)`.
Higher `z` covers lower parts and drops a one-pixel contact shadow onto them
below-right (the light is top-left). `step` moves the whole part along its
ramp. Modes:

- `plate` - flat top, top/left edges lit, bottom/right edges dark (bevel 1-2).
- `inset` - recessed (reverse bevel): hatches, bays, sockets.
- `facet` - a sloped face, `normal=(nx, ny)`: (0,-0.9) faces the front and
  catches the light, (0, 0.8) faces the rear, (-0.8, 0) faces left (lit).
- `dome` - a hemisphere (`gain` < 1 for a low dome; turrets like 0.5-0.6).
- `cylv` / `cylh` - cylinders along y / x (barrels, tanks, pipes).
- `grille` - slats (`pattern=dict(period=2, dir='h'|'v')`).
- `tread` - a track run that scrolls with the frame (`b.tread(...)`).
- `glass` - dark glass with a glint.
- `flat` - one colour (`step`), `rivet` - a bright bump, `map` - explicit
  offsets.

`b.paint(rows, x0, y0, legend, mirror_x=True)` is the precise tool: each
character of the ASCII rows is a legend entry (`dict(mat=, mode=, z=, ...)`
or a glow `dict(glow='marker', kind='marker', z=)`), one part per character;
with `mirror_x` you draw the left half and it is mirrored (a facet's normal is
flipped for the right half automatically). Use it for bespoke shapes.

Helpers: `b.tread(x0,x1,y0,y1,side,z,style='std'|'block'|'fine'|'chevron'|'pad',
period=4)`, `b.barrel(xc2, width, y_tip, y_base, mat=STEEL, z, brake=0,
recoil=self.recoil(pose, i, n))` (xc2 is twice the barrel's centre x: 0 for a
centred even-width barrel, -6/+6 for twins at +-3, -10/+10 for +-5),
`b.lamp(px, role, z, kind=, dir=, housing=)`, `b.rivets(pts, mat, z)`,
`b.grille(...)`, `b.hazard(px, z)`, `b.glass(px, z)`, `b.dome(px, mat, z,
gain=)`, `b.antenna(x, y0, y1, z)`, `b.cut(px)` (notch every part so far).

### Materials

`b.ctx.body` and `b.ctx.accent` are the chassis's own ramps - **always use
them for paint**, never a literal colour, because the player versions swap
them for the team ramps (sky blue for P1, hot pink for P2). Fixed materials:
`STEEL`, `GUNMETAL`, `DARK`, `TRACK`, `PALE`, `GLASS`, `SMOKED`, `BRASS`,
`COPPER`, `RUST`, `HAZARD`, `REDM`, `TEALM`. Palette constants are in
`kit.py` (Puny palette + two interpolated darks). Every colour must come
from a material or a palette constant - nothing off-palette.

### Light (emissive) roles

`b.lamp(px, role, ...)` draws a glowing fixture that also goes into the
emissive layer (what shines at night). Roles: `lamp` (headlight - warm white
on players, amber on enemies), `tail` (red), `marker` (the chassis accent
glow on enemies, **the team colour on players** - the strongest identity cue
at night), `sensor` (red eye on enemies, team colour on players), `beacon`
(amber), `engine` (combustion), `ion` (blue-white), `core` (reactor white),
`plasma`, `laser`, `fire`, `hot`, `white`. Register lights for the night
render with `kind=`: `head` (cone forward), `spot` (turret searchlight,
follows the aim), `tail`, `marker`, `beacon`, `sensor`. Every tank needs
headlights (hull, `kind='head'`), tail lights, a turret sensor, and a
distinctive marker/accent light signature.

### Tags (they drive the damage engine - tag carefully)

`armor` - a plate that can be blown off to show the frame (small and medium
pieces; never the whole hull or turret shell - they come off smallest
first), `skirt` (a chunk tears away), `stowage` (lost first), `antenna` and
`fragile` (snap at tier 2), `hatch` (open on the broken turret), `optic`,
`track` (automatic for treads), `exhaust`, `vent`, `pad`, `ring` (the turret
ring the `blown` wreck tears out). Glow parts are broken in a planned order;
one survives at tier 3 as a blinking red warning lamp.

### Design class

    @LINE.design('breaker')
    class Breaker(Design):
        codename = 'Anvil'            # a one-word name
        blurb = '...'                 # one sentence: what it is, what to notice
        locomotion = 'tracks'         # tracks | hover | quad
        marks = 'tread'               # tread | heavy | wash | quad
        hover = 0                     # 2 for hovering hulls (shadow sits further out)
        internal_glow = 'fire'        # what shows through blown plates at tier 3: fire | ion
        def hull(self, b, f): ...     # f = animation frame 0-3 (treads scroll, pads pulse)
        def turret(self, b, pose): ...  # pose 0 rest, 1 first barrel recoiled, 2 second / return
        def hardpoints(self, ctx):    # turret-local anchors for the line's weapon modules
            return dict(minigun=(x, y), laser=(x, y), missiles=(x, y), flame=(x, y),
                        plasma=[(muzzle_x, muzzle_y), ...])

Set `b.meta['muzzles'] = [(x, y_tip), ...]` in `turret()`. Module functions
(`@LINE.module_fn('minigun')` etc.) draw a module at `hp[...]` for a given
state (minigun 0 idle / 1-3 hot barrel; missiles 0-4 tubes empty; plasma,
laser 0 idle / 1 charged / 2 firing; flame 0-1 pilot / 2-3 firing). A module
must sit well on every turret of the line - adjust each design's hardpoints.

The hull's outer box **including the outline** must stay within `fp` (the
outline adds one pixel, so parts stay one pixel inside). A lamp or antenna
may poke one pixel out. Twin barrels stay at the listed lateral offsets
(shells spawn there); muzzle tips may move up to 2 px from today's. The
turret body is today's size: about 9-10 px across on standard chassis, 13-15
on the super-heavies; it may be any shape the line calls for. Colour
identities (`body`/`accent` in `kit.CHASSIS`) are fixed.

## What we learned drawing the references (read this twice)

1. **Colour identity must dominate.** Today's tanks are 100 % body colour;
   the first refit draft was 45 % grey and read as a grey tank with an
   orange hat. Keep the body colour on roughly half or more of the visible
   silhouette; greys are for metal parts (barrels, mantlets, treads, vents).
   Cover the inner part of each track with a body-coloured lip or skirt.
2. **No big dark masses.** Grilles, racks and recesses darken fast at 2x.
   Keep them small, and lift them a step (`step=1`) when they must be large.
3. **No noise.** Details are clusters of 2+ pixels. A lone pixel is only a
   lamp, a rivet or a glint. If a region looks speckled, simplify it.
4. **Twin barrels need air**: at least two transparent pixels between their
   outlines. Brakes that close the gap turn two guns into a black fork.
5. **Barrels are light steel** (`STEEL`, not `GUNMETAL`): the cylinder
   shading already darkens the right side.
6. **Domes need `gain` 0.5-0.6** or they shade into dark balls.
7. **One focal point per layer** - a glowing sensor, a canopy, a reactor -
   plus a clear light signature at night (check the `night` state).
8. **Silhouette first.** Every chassis must be recognisable in silhouette
   inside the line (scout vs wraith vs glacier are all small - make them
   unmistakable), and every line must be recognisable across chassis.
9. **Check at game scale**, not only zoomed: `render.py preview <line>`
   shows the roster at 2x on the real grass. The owner will judge there.
10. **Check the teams** (`p1`, `p2`): body and accent become the team ramps;
    make sure the player tanks still look designed, and that `marker` lights
    carry the identity.
11. **Check the damage** (`damaged`, `critical`, wrecks): if a tier looks
    wrong it is usually your tags (untagged plates never come off; a huge
    `armor` part leaves a huge hole).

## Process

1. Read `kit.py` (the API) and your line file (the reference assault).
2. Sketch each chassis's concept in a comment first: role, silhouette,
   signature feature(s) in your line's language, the light signature.
3. Draw it, render `hero` and `zoom`, look, refine - at least two passes per
   tank. Then the modules on it (`mods` state).
4. When all 12 exist, render `preview <line>` and look at the line as a
   whole: consistent language, distinct silhouettes, balanced colour.
   Fix the weakest two tanks.
5. Only edit `lines/<your line>.py`. If you need a kit feature, write a
   helper in your file. Report kit bugs rather than patching shared files.

Report at the end: per chassis, the codename and one line on the concept;
anything you could not solve; any kit bugs.
