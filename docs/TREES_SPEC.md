# Trees — `static/trees_sheet.png`

Two destructible tree species, generated by `tools/spritegen/gen_trees.py`
(`SPRITE_OUT=static python3 tools/spritegen/gen_trees.py`). They are
`obstacle::Material` variants like everything else a map can make solid, so
physics, the hit sweep, damage, breach AI, blast chaining, the nav grid and
`maplint` all work on them without a line of new code.

---

## 1. Why the cell is 48px when every other obstacle is 32

Everything in this game holds one invariant: **one design pixel is two
screen pixels.** Ground is 16px art at scale 2.0, obstacles are 32px art at
scale 1.0 with a 2× block grid baked in, tanks are 32px at scale 2.0.

A tree wants to be *bigger than a wall tile* — one 32px cell reads as
masonry with leaves on it — but its collider has to stay one cell, or the
nav grid, the map format and `OBSTACLE_GRID_SIZE` all move with it.
Scaling a 32px tile up would have solved the size and broken the density
(1 art px = 1.5 or 2 screen px, visibly chunkier than everything around
it). So the *cell* grew instead and the pixels did not:

| | source cell | GRID | design px | draw scale | screen | screen px per design px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wall / prop | 32 | 2 | 16 | 1.0 | 32 | 2 |
| **tree** | **48** | **2** | **24** | **1.0** | **48** | **2** |
| tank | 32 | (baked) | 32 | 2.0 | 64 | 2 |

The extra 8 screen px on each side is canopy hanging over the neighbouring
cells. That overhang is the point: it is what stops a grove reading as a
grid of identical squares, and it is why a tree is drawn in the vegetation
pass rather than with the walls (§4).

`Obstacle::size()` stays the cell (collider, grid, map); `Obstacle::
sprite_size()` is the drawn size. Never use the latter for anything but
drawing.

## 2. Sheet layout

768 × 480 — 16 columns × 10 rows of 48px cells.

| col | 0–11 | 12 | 13–15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| meaning | `frame * 3 + stage` | stump | burn 1–3 |

The three `Material::visible_stages` (pristine, thinning, nearly bare)
appear once per **dapple frame** — see §4.1, that cycle is a tree's whole
animation. Column 12 is the terminal stump: like every other material's
terminal cell it is **never drawn** — `destroyed` fires the same frame
health reaches zero and the entity is gone that frame — and exists so the
sheet is complete. Columns 13–15 are the burn loop.

Trees are the one material whose columns are not `Obstacle::col`'s layout,
so `obstacle::tree_col` computes them instead and `draw_tree` is the only
thing that draws a tree.

| rows | contents |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | `Material::Tree` — broadleaf, 4 cosmetic variants |
| 4–7 | `Material::Pine` — conifer, 4 cosmetic variants |
| 8 | rubble: leaf litter and snapped branches (`RUBBLE_ROW_TREE`) |
| 9 | the same, burnt out (`RUBBLE_ROW_TREE_CHARRED`) |

The rubble rows carry `RUBBLE_VARIANTS` (8) columns of rising coverage, the
same convention as the walls sheet's rubble block, and `draw_decal`'s
mirror plus quarter-turn takes that to 32 apparent forms per row.

**Tree rubble is the one leftover not on `walls_sheet.png`.** Every other
material's rubble lives there, props included. Leaf litter is green, and
the walls sheet is under the no-green guard (`just check-sheets`), so
`Material::rubble_row` returns the *sheet* along with the row and `Decal`
carries it.

## 3. The art

Drawn into a 24×24 design-pixel buffer and expanded to 2×2 blocks at the
end, so a stray one-pixel detail is not something the generator can
express — contrast `gen_walls.py`, which draws in sheet pixels and snaps
every primitive onto the block grid by hand. There is no `pixelate()` pass
here because there is nothing for it to catch.

- **Broadleaf** — six to eight overlapping leaf lobes, each with its own
  highlight up-left (the game's light direction, `shadow_dir_*`) and a dark
  arc along its own shaded side so the crown reads as clumps rather than
  one green dome.
- **Conifer** — three rings of needle sprays stepping inward, dark at the
  rim, with only the crown tip catching light. Narrower and darker than the
  broadleaf, so the two are told apart by silhouette before colour.
- **Foliage uses an explicit four-step ramp**, never `mul()`: scaling a
  colour and snapping it back crosses palette families, which is how a
  darkened sand tone once came out green (docs/PALETTE.md).
- **Damage** erodes the canopy from the rim inward and reveals a branch
  skeleton that was drawn *under* the foliage from the start — a wounded
  tree shows its own branches rather than an arbitrary new sprite.
- **Burning** is mostly soot and ash with two small flames on top. A canopy
  recoloured wholesale onto the fire ramp stops reading as a tree at all;
  the first pass looked like a fireball with a trunk.

Green is allowed here, as on `nature_sheet.png`: **manufactured objects are
never green; vegetation is.** `tools/check_sheets.py` lists both sheets as
exceptions and still holds them to the palette (the extended set — trees
use `GREEN_SHADE` and `WOOD_ASH`).

## 4. Behaviour

| | Tree (broadleaf) | Pine (conifer) |
|---|---|---|
| health | `tree_max_health` (12) | `pine_max_health` (9) |
| blocks sight | yes | yes |
| pass-over / deflect | none | none |
| ram | `tree_ram_seconds` (0.5 s) | same |
| catches fire | `tree_flammable_chance` (0.55) | same |
| burn timing | `wood_burn_seconds` | same |

- **Brittle, in the fence bracket.** A tree is not a wall with leaves on
  it: one shell normally fells one. The numbers are set from the *weakest*
  chassis — a scout's shell rolls 7.5–22.5 (`player_damage_min..max` times
  its `tank_damage_factor`) and clears 12 about 70% of the time, the same
  one-shot rate `fence_one_shot_chance` gives a fence, arrived at through
  damage rather than a coin flip. Everything heavier fells one every time.
  Keeping the health model rather than copying the fence's amount-blind
  rule is what makes a minigun burst still have to chew through a trunk
  and a barrel blast still take a whole stand down at once.
- **Ramming.** A tank leaning on a tree pushes it over after
  `tree_ram_seconds`, a beat longer than a sandbag and well under
  `enemy_breach_after_seconds`, so an AI that drives into one pushes
  through instead of stopping to shoot it.
- **Fire.** A tree rolls flammable at spawn exactly the way Wood does, and
  burns through the same `Obstacle::tick_burn`. A zero chance draws no RNG
  at all, so a treeless map replays bit-for-bit as it did before trees
  existed. **A rammed tree is the exception**: it dies outright rather than
  taking the flammable fork, because a hull pushing a trunk over is not
  something that sets it alight — and without that rule a tank driving
  through a wood would leave a trail of fires behind it.
- **Seams.** Trees are excluded from seam-closing
  (`battlefield::tile_half_extent`) in **both** physics and the projectile
  hit test: a grove is trunks with gaps between them, not a slab, and a
  shot threading that gap is the right outcome. Both paths go through the
  one function so they cannot disagree.
- **No edge cap.** The blob autotile (`draw_obstacle_cap`) is for wall runs
  only; `Material::is_wall` gates it.
- **Draw order.** Trees are held out of the obstacle pass and drawn with
  the tall grass, over the tanks and under the projectiles, sorted back to
  front by `y`. Over the tanks so a tank pressed against a trunk has the
  canopy overhanging it; sorted because 48px sprites overlap on a 32px grid
  and an unordered query makes a grove's crowns pop in and out of each
  other.

## 4.1 Dapple, and leaning over

**A tree does not sway.** An ambient bend was built and rejected: what
reads as alive on a blade of grass reads as wrong on a crown, because a
trunk is stiff and the eye knows it. What a tree does instead is *change
colour* — the geometry never moves at all.

**Dapple** (`obstacle::tree_col`). Every damage stage exists in
`TREE_SHIMMER_FRAMES` copies that are the same canopy with three small
patches of leaf stepped one rung up the foliage ramp; the patches drift a
design pixel or so between frames, so cycling them is light moving through
the leaves. Two details make it read as light rather than as noise:

- The patches are seeded from the tree itself, so every frame lights the
  *same* places and only where they sit moves.
- Inside a patch only some pixels step, on a **position**-keyed hash rather
  than a frame-keyed one — the speckle stays put while the patch slides over
  it, which is how light through leaves actually behaves. It must be a hash
  and not a parity test: `(x + y) % 2` and its relatives lay a visible
  diagonal weave across the whole crown.

The step is one rung and never off `RIM`, so the silhouette is untouched:
frame 0 differs from the un-dappled art by about 5% of the canopy and
consecutive frames by 2–5%. `tree_dapple_seconds` sets the rate and the
tree's position hash sets its phase, so a wood shimmers out of step with
itself rather than blinking as one.

**The lean** is the one time a tree's geometry moves, and only while a tank
is pushing it over. `Obstacle::ram_timer` is already exactly "how long
something has been leaning on this", so the bend grows with its square and
the tree is well over by the time it goes — without it, ramming a tree read
as nothing happening: it stood bolt upright and then vanished. Direction is
away from the nearest live tank; a push straight along the trunk's axis has
no sideways component to give, so it falls back to a side chosen from the
tree's own hash, which holds still for the whole push.

`tree_blit` applies that bend as eight horizontal bands each shifted by a
whole **2px block** — the same grid `fx.rs` snaps particles to. It is
deliberately not a rotation: rotating the sprite about its base (what grass
does) re-samples the crown's interior every frame, and measured against a
static capture the whole inside of every canopy churned, not just its
outline. Bands that land on the same block coalesce, so a tree nothing is
touching costs a single ordinary quad.

## 5. Maps

Two new cell kinds, `{ kind = "tree" }` and `{ kind = "pine" }`, and two
palette icons in the map editor (`editor.rs`). Both are solid, so
`CellObject::is_solid` folds them in with walls and props everywhere a
solid tile matters.

The editor's palette wraps into rows now (`MapEditor::palette_columns`) —
23 icons no longer fit across 1280px in one line.
